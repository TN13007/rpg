# Character Progression Design

**Status:** Directional; detailed progression rules remain open  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-21

## Purpose

Progression should expand what the student and their companions can do across ordinary life, investigation and dangerous encounters. A stronger character should not merely deal a larger number; they should gain new options, knowledge, combinations or resilience.

## Candidate dimensions

| Dimension | Design responsibility | Desired player impact |
|---|---|---|
| Attributes | Broad character capabilities | Change underlying strengths and weaknesses |
| Equipment | Practical tools and build specialization | Change action properties, defense or tactical trade-offs |
| Skills | Practical and tactical options | Add or modify actions, effects and combinations |
| Mastery / proficiency | Focused expertise | Reward deliberate use of a capability where appropriate |
| Knowledge | Understanding of people, places and anomalies | Help the player make better-informed decisions |
| Relationships / support | Social and character development | Create meaningful assistance, conflicts or opportunities |

These are candidate dimensions, not a commitment to implement six separate systems. Avoid overlap: each system must own a distinct player-facing outcome.

## Principles

1. Prefer new capabilities over raw stat inflation.
2. Make equipment and builds involve meaningful trade-offs.
3. Preserve distinct character identity.
4. Support multiple viable approaches to an incident.
5. Make growth readable: the player should understand what changed and how to use it.
6. Introduce only the progression dimensions required by the current playable slice.

## Student life and personal development

University activities may provide learning, practice, research, recovery and access to classmates or mentors. Part-time work may provide money and contacts. Personal routines can create opportunities to develop capabilities, but they should not become a separate management game.

## Paranormal knowledge

Knowledge may help the player recognize patterns, assess risk or choose a safer response. Do not turn every anomaly into a combat stat check. Information, preparation and professional assistance should remain viable alternatives where appropriate.

## Open decisions

Which attributes are active and what each changes; whether levels, activity-linked growth or milestones drive progression; how practical skills are learned and equipped; whether Mastery is necessary; how equipment changes action timing and options; and how relationships mechanically affect investigation or combat.

## Body Profile progression

Body Profile is a candidate long-term character progression dimension. The selected direction is that body attributes can develop over time rather than remaining fixed constants. This direction is accepted; the attributes, growth sources, rates and trade-offs are not.

Candidate regions include Mouth, Hands, Chest, Genitals, Anus and Skin. Candidate attributes include Sensitivity, Resilience and Familiarity. Do not assume every region needs every attribute. Each attribute must have a distinct gameplay effect and a clear reason to exist.

Keep three kinds of data separate:

- **Persistent Body Profile:** attributes that can change through long-term progression.
- **Current Body State:** temporary Exposure, Accessibility and encounter effects.
- **Enemy Preference Profile:** an enemy-owned tendency that influences its choice among valid actions; it is not a player progression stat.

Growth should create meaningful differences or trade-offs rather than universally increasing every value. Possible sources such as training, authored experiences and milestones remain candidates until validated in a playable slice. Body Profile values are not intended to appear as numeric combat UI meters.

The relationship between Body Profile and combat Arousal/Satisfaction resolution is owned by [Combat Design](Combat.md). Progression must not independently calculate combat outcomes.
