# Character Progression Design

**Status:** Directional; detailed progression rules remain open  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-20

## Purpose

Progression should make the party more capable and give the player new ways to solve combat and adventure problems. A stronger character should not merely deal a larger number; they should gain new options, combinations, specializations or resilience.

## Progression dimensions

| Dimension | Design responsibility | Desired player impact |
|---|---|---|
| Attributes | Broad character capabilities | Change underlying strengths and weaknesses |
| Equipment | Build specialization | Change action properties, scaling, defense or tactical trade-offs |
| Skills | Tactical options | Add or modify actions, effects and combinations |
| Mastery / proficiency | Focused expertise | Reward repeated or deliberate use of a combat style where appropriate |
| Knowledge | Understanding of enemies, places or techniques | Help the player make better-informed decisions |
| Relationships / support | Character and story development | Create meaningful assistance, conflicts or opportunities |

These are candidate dimensions, not a commitment to implement six separate progression systems. Avoid overlap: each system must own a distinct player-facing outcome.

## Design principles

1. **New capability over raw inflation:** prioritize new actions, effects and synergies over generic stat increases.
2. **Meaningful trade-offs:** equipment and builds should have strengths and costs.
3. **Distinct character identity:** characters should not become interchangeable after progression.
4. **Multiple viable builds:** avoid one mandatory stat allocation or skill path.
5. **Readable growth:** the player should understand what changed and how to use it.
6. **Controlled complexity:** introduce only the progression dimensions required by the current playable slice.

## Academy relationship

Academy activities may provide training, skill development, research, recovery and access to mentors or companions. These activities support character growth but should not become a separate management game that competes with adventure and combat.

## Reincarnation and long-term growth

The broader story may use reincarnation and continuity across lives as a narrative and progression theme. The exact retained knowledge, resets, inherited capabilities and mechanical effects remain open. Do not define a reincarnation economy before the first-life experience and core progression loop are proven.

## Open decisions

- Which attributes are active and what each one changes.
- Whether levels, activity-linked growth or milestone growth are the primary progression mechanism.
- How skills are learned, upgraded and equipped.
- Whether Mastery is necessary or can be represented through skills/attributes.
- How equipment changes timing and action options.
- What persists or resets across reincarnations.
- How relationships mechanically affect combat, if at all.
