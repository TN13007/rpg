# Game Vision — Modern Student Life RPG

**Status:** Revised product direction  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-23

## 1. High concept

A life-simulation RPG about an ordinary university student living in a contemporary city. The player manages everyday life, develops characters, builds relationships, forms a party and participates in a range of activities—from familiar student routines to specialized challenges and dangerous encounters.

The game combines the life-simulation structure and player freedom of *Degrees of Lewdity* with the party-based RPG and character-interaction direction of *Karryn's Prison*. A defining emphasis is the practical influence of character skills: development should change what characters can do and how effectively they perform, rather than only increasing numerical attributes.

The experience is built in layers. Foundational systems support everyday activities. Everyday activities establish context, resources and progression for more specialized gameplay. Further layers introduce deeper interactions, challenges and systems that expand the player's possibilities.

The complete experience is the combination of these layers. No single loop represents the entire game. The foundation appears most frequently because every higher layer depends on it.

The protagonist is an ordinary student, not a chosen one or a predetermined hero. The world contains ordinary people, recognizable places and unexplained phenomena. The player decides how to spend time, which relationships to pursue, which activities to engage in and how much of the unusual side of the world to explore.

## 2. Core experience

The player moves through a continuous experience that combines everyday life with increasingly specialized activities.

The experience is built around five principles:

1. **A persistent everyday foundation:** Time, character state, resources, locations and actions provide the basis for the entire experience.
2. **Layered gameplay:** Each gameplay layer builds upon existing systems instead of replacing or duplicating them.
3. **Player freedom:** The player chooses activities, priorities and approaches rather than following a mandatory sequence of content.
4. **Character-driven gameplay:** Attributes, skills, resources and development meaningfully influence what the player can accomplish.
5. **Interconnected progression:** Outcomes from one activity can influence opportunities and decisions in other activities.

Daily life and specialized gameplay are not separate games. They are different layers of the same experience.

## 3. Layered gameplay architecture

The game is organized into progressively developed gameplay loops. Each loop inherits the systems and capabilities established by the previous layers, then introduces additional gameplay to expand the experience.

### Loop 1 — Foundation

The foundation establishes the rules through which the player, characters and world operate.

It contains systems used repeatedly across the entire game:

- **Time:** The passage of time and resolution of time-consuming actions.
- **Character:** Character identity, attributes, skills, resources and current state.
- **Location:** Where characters are and how locations connect.
- **Actions:** Available actions, their requirements, costs and outcomes.
- **Resources:** Money, stamina and other resources required by activities.
- **Progression:** Changes to character capabilities and state over time.

This layer does not represent a complete gameplay experience by itself. It provides the shared foundation that allows higher-level gameplay to function consistently.

A character performing an everyday activity, participating in a social interaction or fighting in combat still relies on the underlying character and resource rules. Specialized activities may introduce their own resolution rules, but should reuse shared concepts where appropriate.

### Loop 2 — Daily Life

Daily Life builds upon the foundation to create the player's ordinary routine.

The player uses time, locations, characters, actions and resources to organize life and pursue personal goals.

Activities may include:

- Attending classes and studying.
- Working part-time jobs.
- Managing money and personal expenses.
- Resting and recovering.
- Visiting shops and public locations.
- Meeting friends and other characters.
- Exploring the city.
- Spending time at home.
- Developing personal skills and attributes.

Daily activities provide more than temporary rewards. They establish the player's routine, create opportunities, develop character capabilities and determine the resources available for other activities.

The player should have meaningful reasons to engage with everyday life even when no specialized event is active.

Daily Life is not a mandatory sequence of tasks. It is a flexible gameplay layer in which the player decides how to spend available time and resources.

### Loop 3 — Specialized Activities

Specialized Activities build upon Daily Life and the Foundation to introduce deeper gameplay that cannot be adequately represented by ordinary daily actions alone.

Each activity has its own rules, interactions, decisions and progression. It may use shared systems such as character attributes, skills, resources, time and location while maintaining ownership of its specialized mechanics.

Potential activity domains include:

**Social Interaction**
- Conversations and dialogue choices.
- Developing friendships and relationships.
- Understanding character preferences and circumstances.
- Building or damaging relationships through player actions.

**Combat**
- Party-based tactical encounters.
- Selecting characters and using their available skills.
- Managing shared party resources and individual character capabilities.
- Responding to enemy patterns and encounter conditions.
- Using character development and party composition to influence outcomes.

**Romance and Intimacy**
- Developing personal relationships.
- Character-specific interactions and preferences.
- Progression through relationship-related activities.
- Interactions influenced by character attributes, skills and previous outcomes.

**Investigation**
- Discovering rumors and unusual situations.
- Gathering information and examining evidence.
- Choosing whether to investigate, leave, seek help or confront a threat.
- Resolving situations through different approaches.

**Recreation and Other Activities**
- Leisure activities and hobbies.
- Optional challenges and specialized interactions.
- Activities that provide alternative ways to develop characters or relationships.

These domains are not required to share one universal activity system. Each domain owns the rules necessary to deliver its gameplay.

Specialized activities should expand the player's options without making ordinary life irrelevant.

### Loop 4+ — Extended Gameplay

Further layers combine and extend the capabilities of previous loops. They may introduce more complex interactions between characters, activities, progression and world state.

Potential directions include:

- Advanced party management and character specialization.
- Deeper skill development and skill combinations.
- More complex relationship progression.
- Activities with interconnected requirements and outcomes.
- Long-term character and party development.
- Advanced encounters and specialized challenges.
- More persistent consequences across different gameplay domains.

These layers should be introduced when the existing gameplay provides a clear reason for them. They are not prerequisites for establishing the initial experience.

## 4. How the loops work together

The loops are cumulative layers, not independent gameplay modes.

The foundation is used most frequently because every activity depends on it. Daily Life adds a repeatable context for using the foundation. Specialized Activities introduce additional rules and decisions that expand what the player can experience. Further layers combine these capabilities into more complex gameplay.

Conceptually:

```text
                 LOOP 4+
       Extended and interconnected gameplay
                       |
                       v
                 LOOP 3
             Specialized activities
                       |
                       v
                 LOOP 2
                 Daily life
                       |
                       v
                 LOOP 1
                  Foundation
```

This hierarchy describes system dependency and gameplay construction, not a mandatory order of player actions.

The player does not need to finish Loop 2 before entering Loop 3. Instead, Loop 3 relies on the capabilities and context established by the lower layers.

For example:

- Character attributes and skills are established by the foundation.
- Daily activities provide opportunities to develop those capabilities.
- Combat uses character capabilities and introduces specialized tactical rules.
- Combat outcomes may affect character state, resources, relationships or later opportunities.
- Those changes become part of the player's ongoing experience.

The same principle applies to social interactions, investigation, romance and other activity domains.

## 5. Character and skill-driven gameplay

Characters are central to the RPG experience.

Attributes describe general character capabilities, while skills represent more specific abilities that influence gameplay.

Character development should produce observable differences in how the player approaches activities.

Skills should not exist only as numerical progression. They should have practical effects, such as:

- Unlocking additional actions or interaction options.
- Improving the effectiveness of existing actions.
- Changing action requirements or resource costs.
- Providing advantages in specialized activities.
- Creating different tactical possibilities.
- Enabling character specialization and complementary party roles.

Attributes and skills should have clear responsibilities. Attributes may provide general capabilities, while skills provide more specialized effects and gameplay options.

The exact relationship between attributes, skills and individual activity domains should be defined by their respective design documents.

## 6. Party-based RPG

The game uses a party-based character structure for specialized gameplay, particularly combat.

The player develops and manages multiple characters rather than relying exclusively on a single protagonist.

Party gameplay should create meaningful decisions around:

- Character selection and party composition.
- Individual strengths and weaknesses.
- Skill availability and specialization.
- Shared and individual resources.
- Complementary abilities.
- Character development and encounter strategy.

Characters should not become interchangeable collections of statistics. Their capabilities and skills should create distinct gameplay roles.

The party system should also support the broader life-simulation experience. Characters and relationships should remain part of the world beyond combat encounters.

## 7. Combat direction

Combat is a specialized turn-based gameplay domain.

It uses party decisions, character skills, resources and authored enemy behavior to create tactical encounters.

The current combat direction uses:

- Discrete Player Turns and Enemy Turns.
- A shared Action Point pool for the party.
- Individual character action restrictions.
- Authored enemy action patterns.
- Target selection when required by an action.
- Character resources and skills as part of action resolution.

Combat is not the universal answer to every unusual situation. The player may investigate, avoid confrontation, retreat or seek help when those responses are available.

Combat should reward character development and tactical decisions. Party composition, skill selection and resource management should meaningfully influence encounter outcomes.

The detailed combat rules belong in the Combat Design documentation rather than this high-level vision.

## 8. World and setting

The game takes place in a contemporary city centered around university student life.

The university is the primary social and routine anchor. The surrounding city provides locations for housing, shopping, employment, recreation, social interactions and unusual encounters.

Important locations may include:

- University campus and facilities.
- Student apartments and residential areas.
- Part-time workplaces.
- Shops and commercial districts.
- Public spaces and recreational locations.
- Places connected to unusual events.
- Professional organizations that respond to paranormal situations.

The world should remain recognizable as a contemporary setting.

Unexplained phenomena exist alongside ordinary life, but they do not turn the entire setting into a fantasy world.

Phones, laptops, cameras, messaging applications and the Internet are ordinary tools. They may support communication, information gathering and decision-making without requiring a supernatural explanation.

The protagonist is not required to investigate every unusual event or follow a central heroic storyline.

## 9. Optional paranormal content

Paranormal incidents provide another dimension of gameplay built upon the existing life-simulation structure.

They may appear through rumors, observations, messages, conversations or direct encounters.

The player should be able to choose how to respond:

- Ignore the situation.
- Observe or gather information.
- Investigate further.
- Document evidence.
- Leave the area.
- Seek professional assistance.
- Confront a threat when appropriate.

Not every incident needs to be dangerous. Some may be harmless, strange, inconvenient or humorous.

Consequences should be proportionate to the situation. Ignoring an incident may change a later opportunity or event, but not every unresolved situation needs to become a catastrophe.

Paranormal content should expand the player's experience rather than replace ordinary student life.

## 10. Shared systems and domain ownership

Gameplay layers reuse shared systems but retain responsibility for their own rules.

Shared systems may include:

- Character data and runtime state.
- Attributes and skills.
- Time and resource management.
- Location and navigation.
- Inventory and equipment.
- Character progression.

Specialized domains may extend or interpret these systems according to their requirements.

For example, Daily Life may consume time and resources through ordinary actions, while Combat may use its own turn and action-resolution rules.

A shared design model does not imply that every domain must use one universal interaction executor or activity dispatcher.

Each gameplay domain should own its validation, execution and outcome rules. Shared infrastructure should be introduced only when multiple domains genuinely require it.

## 11. Development principles

### Build from the foundation upward

Establish the systems required by the lowest layer before introducing higher-level dependencies.

### Validate complete experiences, not isolated mechanics

A feature should be tested as part of a player journey. A functional action or combat encounter alone does not demonstrate that the surrounding gameplay works.

### Keep the layers interconnected

Higher-level activities should use and influence lower-level systems where appropriate. Avoid creating parallel versions of character, time or resource logic without a clear reason.

### Give skills practical value

Character progression should change gameplay possibilities and effectiveness, not only increase displayed numbers.

### Preserve player freedom

The player should have meaningful choices about activities, priorities and approaches. Optional content should remain optional unless a specific authored experience requires otherwise.

### Prefer focused systems over premature simulation

Build only the behavior and state needed to create observable decisions. Do not introduce broad simulation frameworks before gameplay demonstrates their value.

### Expand through concrete gameplay needs

A new layer or system should solve a recognizable limitation in the existing experience and provide a meaningful addition to the player journey.

## 12. Content and development priorities

Development should proceed by establishing the foundation and progressively building complete gameplay experiences on top of it.

**Foundation**
- Time and action resolution.
- Character data, attributes, skills and resources.
- Location and navigation.
- Shared character state and progression.

**Daily Life**
- A coherent student routine.
- Classes, studying and part-time work.
- Money, rest and resource management.
- Basic interactions with people and locations.

**Specialized Activities**
- Social interaction and relationships.
- Party-based turn-based combat.
- Skill-driven character interactions.
- Optional paranormal investigation and encounters.
- Romance, intimacy and other activities according to content priorities.

**Extended Gameplay**
- Deeper party and character specialization.
- More interconnected activity outcomes.
- Advanced progression and relationship systems.
- Additional specialized gameplay domains.

The order is a development dependency, not a restriction on the eventual player experience. A higher layer may be prototyped early when doing so helps validate its requirements, but its dependencies must be identified and supported.

## 13. Scope evaluation

Evaluate a proposed feature using the following questions:

1. Which gameplay layer does this feature belong to?
2. Which existing systems does it depend on?
3. Does it extend an existing capability or introduce a genuinely new one?
4. Does it create a meaningful player decision or gameplay outcome?
5. Do character attributes, skills or party composition meaningfully affect it where appropriate?
6. Does it interact with other gameplay layers in a useful way?
7. Can its value be demonstrated through a small, complete player journey?
8. Is a new runtime system necessary, or can existing domain-owned rules support it?

A feature should be deferred when it adds complexity without meaningfully expanding the player's experience.

## 14. Product identity

The intended game is a combination of:

- The freedom and everyday-life simulation structure of *Degrees of Lewdity*.
- The party-based RPG and character-interaction direction of *Karryn's Prison*.
- A stronger emphasis on skills as practical gameplay capabilities.
- A layered architecture in which everyday systems support increasingly specialized gameplay.

The defining experience is not simply attending university, managing a schedule or participating in combat.

It is the combination of everyday life, character development, relationships, party gameplay and specialized activities within one interconnected experience.

**The foundation makes the world function. Daily Life gives the player a reason to live in it. Specialized Activities give the player more ways to engage with it. Further layers deepen the possibilities.**
